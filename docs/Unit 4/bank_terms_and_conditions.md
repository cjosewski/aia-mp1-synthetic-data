The law requires us to know who our customers are. Before we open an account, and from time to time afterwards, we will ask you for:

(a) your full name, date of birth, nationality and home address;

(b) an official photo document, such as a passport, a driving licence or a national identity card;

A joint account is an account in the names of two or more people. Unless you tell us otherwise in writing when the account is opened:

(a) each of you can use the account alone, give instructions, withdraw the whole balance and ask for a card, without the agreement of the others;

(b) each of you is responsible, separately and together, for everything that is owed on the account, whoever caused the debt. We can ask any one of you to repay the whole amount;

If you would like instructions to need the signature of all account holders, you must ask for this when the account is opened or change the account mandate later. Cards and digital services may not be available on an account that needs more than one signature.

Any one of you may ask to be removed from the account, but you remain liable for debts that arose while you were a holder. When one joint holder dies, the balance belongs to the surviving holder or holders.

You can allow another person to operate your account for you. We call this person your representative.

There are two ways to appoint a representative. You can sign our third-party mandate form, which gives a named person the right to give instructions on the account. We need to see the original document or a certified copy, and we will check the identity of the representative in the same way as we checked yours. We may refuse a power of attorney that is unclear, that is more than five years old or that we cannot verify.

A representative can do what the document allows and no more. Unless it says so in clear words, a representative cannot open new accounts in your name, apply for credit, change your address, appoint a further representative or close the account.

You are bound by what your representative does within their authority, also where they act against your wishes. The authority ends when you tell us in writing that you withdraw it, when we receive proof of your death, or on the end date in the document. Until we have had a reasonable time to act on such a notice, we may go on following the representative's instructions.

# Part 5: Deposits and interest

## 5.1 General terms: Paying money in

Money can be paid into your account in cash at a branch or at one of our deposit ATMs, by cheque, by transfer from another account, or by your employer or another payer directly.

Cash paid in at a branch counter during opening hours is added to your account and available at once. Cash paid in at a deposit ATM is counted by the machine and is normally available at once; if the machine cannot count a note, the deposit is checked by hand on the next business day. If our count differs from the amount you entered, our count applies, and we will tell you of the difference.

A transfer that reaches us is added to your account on the day we receive it. We rely on the account number given by the sender. We do not check that the name given with the payment matches the name on your account, although we may do so.

We may refuse a deposit, or ask where the money comes from, where the law requires us to or where we suspect that the money is linked to a crime. We do not accept foreign coins, damaged notes or cheques made out to someone else.

## 5.2 General terms: How cheques are cleared

When you pay in a cheque, we have to collect the money from the bank of the person who wrote it. This is called clearing, and until it is complete the amount of the cheque is not part of your available balance.

During clearing the cheque may appear on your account as a pending entry. You should not rely on the money until the entry is no longer shown as pending. The length of the clearing period for your type of account is given in the fee sections that carry your account's label.

A cheque can be returned unpaid even after the clearing period, for example where it turns out to be forged or altered. If that happens we take the amount back out of your account, also where this makes the account overdrawn, and we tell you. You will then need to take the matter up with the person who gave you the cheque.

We may refuse a cheque that is more than six months old, that is not signed, that has been altered, or on which the amount in words differs from the amount in figures. A cheque in a foreign currency or drawn on a bank abroad takes longer to clear, and the foreign bank may deduct its own charges.

## 5.3 General terms: Interest

Whether an account earns interest, and at what rate, depends on the account type. The rates are shown in our rate sheet, which is available in branches, in the app and on our website.

Where interest is paid, we work it out each day on the cleared balance at the end of that day, using a year of 365 days. We add it to the account once a month unless the product leaflet gives another date. Interest that has been added becomes part of the balance and earns interest itself.

Where you owe us interest, for example on an overdraft, it is worked out in the same way on the amount owed at the end of each day and taken from the account once a month. We tell you the amount before we take it.

Interest rates are variable unless we have agreed a fixed rate with you for a fixed period. A variable rate may go up or down. The section on changes to interest rates explains how and when we tell you.

Interest that we pay you may be taxable. We pay it without deducting tax unless the law requires a deduction, and we report it to the tax authorities where we are obliged to.

## 5.4 General terms: Payments into your account by mistake

Sometimes money arrives in an account that should not have. The sender may have typed a wrong account number, a payment may have been sent twice, or we may have made an error ourselves.

If money is paid into your account by our mistake, we may take it back without asking you first. We will tell you when we have done so. If you have already spent the money, you must repay it, and we may treat the amount as an overdraft until you do.

If another bank tells us that one of its customers sent you a payment by mistake, we will contact you and ask whether you agree to return it. We may hold the amount while we wait for your reply so that it cannot be spent. If you tell us that the money is rightfully yours, we will not return it without your consent or a court order, but we may give your name and address to the other bank so that the sender can pursue the matter with you directly.

Keeping or spending money that you know is not yours may be a criminal offence. If you notice a payment that you cannot explain, please tell us and do not use it.

## 5.5 General terms: Foreign currency

Your account is held in one currency. A payment that arrives in another currency is converted into the currency of the account before it is added, and a payment that you send in another currency is converted before it leaves.

For conversions we use our own exchange rate for the currency concerned at the time we process the payment. Our rate is made up of a market reference rate and a margin. You can ask for the rate before you give an instruction, but rates move during the day and the rate that is applied may differ from one quoted earlier unless we have fixed it for you.

Card transactions are different. They are converted by the card network at the network's rate, not ours.

If a payment in a foreign currency is returned to us, for example because the account details were wrong, we convert it back at the rate on the day of its return. The amount that comes back may therefore be lower or higher than the amount that was sent, and we are not liable for the difference unless the return was caused by our error.

Charges of foreign banks that handle a payment on its way are outside our control and may be deducted from the amount.

# Part 6: Cash and ATMs

This part deals with cash from ATMs: what a withdrawal costs and how much can be taken out in a day. The amounts differ by product, by country and over time, so each section states the accounts it is for.

## 6.1 Branches in Canada: Cash withdrawals at ATMs

A customer of a Canadian branch can withdraw cash free of charge at any ATM that carries our name in Canada.

At an ATM of another bank in Canada the fee depends on the plan. On the Standard plan each withdrawal costs $4. On the Plus plan the first 4 withdrawals in a month are free, and each one after that costs $4. On the Premium plan there is no fee.

At an ATM outside Canada, including one of our own ATMs in the United States, a withdrawal costs $6 plus 2% of the amount, on every plan.

The most a customer can withdraw from ATMs in one day is $600 on the Standard and Plus plans and $2,500 on the Premium plan. All amounts in this section are in Canadian dollars; a withdrawal made in another currency is converted first and then counted towards the limit.

## 6.2 Savings accounts: Withdrawals at ATMs

A saver may link a cash card to a savings account. The cash card works at ATMs only and cannot be used in shops.

The first 6 withdrawals from a savings account in a calendar month are free, wherever they are made, including at another bank's ATM. Transfers out of the account count towards the 6 in the same way as cash. From the next withdrawal in that month, each withdrawal at another bank's ATM costs $2, and each withdrawal at one of our own ATMs costs $1.

No more than $200 can be taken from a savings account at ATMs in one day. A larger amount can be withdrawn at a branch counter with photo identification, or moved to a current account first.

A cash card for a savings account cannot be used at an ATM in another country.

## 6.3 Business accounts: Cash withdrawals and ATM limits

Cash withdrawals with a business debit card are charged as follows.

(a) At one of our own ATMs: no charge.

(b) At an ATM of another bank inside the country: $4.50 per withdrawal on Business Standard. On Business Plus the first 5 withdrawals in a month are free of charge, counted across all cards issued on the account, and $4.50 applies to each later one.

(c) At an ATM in another country: $7 per withdrawal plus 2.5% of the amount.

Each card has its own daily ATM withdrawal limit. The limit is $1,000 per card on Business Standard and $2,500 per card on Business Plus. The business may set a lower limit for a card through online banking, for example for a card held by a junior employee, and may raise it again up to the plan limit.

Cash withdrawn over the counter at a branch is not an ATM withdrawal. It does not count towards the daily limit, and the first $10,000 per month is free.

## 6.4 Credit cards: Cash advances at ATMs

Taking cash out of an ATM with a credit card is a cash advance. It is borrowing, and it costs more than a purchase.

Each cash advance carries a cash advance fee, added to the card balance on the day of the advance: $10 on the Standard Card, $7.50 on the Plus Card and $6 on the Premium Card. The fee is the same at our own ATMs and at the ATMs of other banks, in this country and abroad. For an advance in a foreign currency the foreign transaction fee applies as well.

Interest on a cash advance runs from the day of the advance until the day it is repaid in full. The interest-free period for purchases does not apply.

The daily cash advance limit is $300 on the Standard Card, $700 on the Plus Card and $1,200 on the Premium Card, and never more than the credit that is still available. Buying foreign currency, travellers' cheques or gambling chips with the card is treated as a cash advance.

## 6.5 Personal accounts: Cash withdrawals at ATMs

Withdrawals at our own ATMs are free on every plan.

Withdrawals at another bank's ATM inside the country:

- Standard: $3 per withdrawal.
- Plus: the first 3 withdrawals per month are free, then $3 per withdrawal.
- Premium: free.

Withdrawals at an ATM in another country cost $5 per withdrawal plus 1.5% of the amount withdrawn. On the Premium plan the $5 is waived, and the 1.5% still applies. The free withdrawals of the Plus plan do not apply abroad.

The ATM operator may charge its own fee on top. That fee is shown on the ATM screen and is not ours to refund.

The daily ATM withdrawal limit is $500 on the Standard and Plus plans and $2,000 on the Premium plan. The limit counts all ATM withdrawals made between midnight and midnight, at any ATM. A customer can ask for a temporary higher limit for up to 7 days by calling support.

## 6.7 General terms: ATM errors and retained cards

An ATM may fail to pay out the right amount, may keep your card or may be out of service. This section says what happens then, at any ATM and on any type of account.

# Part 17: Closing an account and ending the agreement

This part sets out how an account is closed by its holder, for each kind of account, and then the cases in which the agreement ends in another way.

## 17.1 Personal accounts: Closing an account

Closing an account is free, with one exception: if the account is closed within 90 days of the day it was opened, an early closure fee of $25 is charged.

Before an account is closed, the balance must be $0 or more, and all pending payments must be settled. The remaining balance is sent by standard domestic transfer, free of charge, to an account the customer names.

## 17.2 Branches in Canada: Closing an account

A customer may close an account at a Canadian branch at any time, in person at the branch, by phone after security questions, or by secure message in online banking.

Closing is free, except in one case. If the account is closed within 90 days of its opening date, we charge an early closure fee of $30, in Canadian dollars. The fee is not charged when the customer closes the account because they do not accept a change that we have announced to these terms or to the fees.

Before closing, the customer should move regular payments to another account and cancel pre-authorised debits. Moving the whole account to another Canadian bank through the interbank transfer service costs $19, which the receiving bank often refunds.

## 17.3 Fee schedule 2024 (replaced): Closing an account

Under this schedule closing an account was free once the account had been open for 120 days. An account closed within 120 days of the day it was opened was charged an early closure fee of $20.

## 17.4 Business accounts: Closing a business account

**Closure by the business.** A business may close its account by an instruction signed in line with the account mandate. We act on the instruction within 5 business days of receiving it, provided that:

(a) any unarranged borrowing and all fees have been paid;

(b) all cards have been cancelled and unused cheques destroyed;

(c) any card terminal or merchant agreement linked to the account has ended.

**Early closure.** Where a business account is closed within 180 days of being opened, an early closure fee of $45 is charged. After 180 days closure is free.

**Closure by us.** We may close a business account on 60 days' written notice without giving a reason, and at once where the law requires it or the business has given us false information.

**Closing balance.** The closing balance is paid to an account in the name of the business. It is not paid to an owner, director or partner personally.

## 17.5 Savings accounts: Closing a savings account

A saver may close a savings account in the app, by phone or at a branch. We work out the interest up to the day before closing and add it to the balance.

If a savings account is closed within 180 days of opening, we charge an early closure fee of $16. The fee covers the cost of opening the account and is taken from the closing balance. From day 181 closing is free. The fee does not apply where the saver moves the whole balance into another savings account with us.

The closing balance, with the interest, is paid to the saver's current account with us or, where there is none, by standard transfer to an account in the saver's own name at another bank. We do not pay a closing balance in cash above $1,000 or to another person.

A savings account with a balance of zero for 12 months is closed by us automatically.

## 17.6 Credit cards: Closing a card account

A cardholder can close a credit card account at any time by calling us or sending a secure message. The card and any additional cards stop working on the day of the request.

Closing the account does not cancel what is owed. The card balance stays payable under the credit agreement, with interest, and the cardholder keeps receiving statements until it has been paid in full. Recurring charges that merchants send after the closing date may still be added, and the cardholder should cancel them with each merchant.

There is no closing fee for an account that has been open for more than 180 days. If the account is closed within 180 days of opening, an early closure fee of $33 is added to the final statement. Any monthly card fee already charged is not returned.

When the balance has reached zero, we confirm the closure in writing and report the account as closed to the credit bureaus.

## 17.7 General terms: When we may close or suspend an account

We may close your account, or end a particular service, by giving you at least two months' written notice. We do not have to give a reason, but we will not close an account for a reason that the law forbids.

We may close or suspend an account at once, without notice, if:

(a) you gave us false or misleading information when you applied or later;

(b) we reasonably believe that the account is being used for fraud, money laundering or another crime, or to receive money obtained by deceiving others;

(c) you have seriously or repeatedly broken these terms;

(d) you have threatened or abused our staff;

(e) keeping the account open would put us in breach of a law, a regulation, a sanction or a court order.

When an account is suspended, payments in and out are stopped, but the money in it remains yours unless a court or authority orders otherwise. Where the law permits, we tell you why and what you can do.

When we close an account, we pay you the balance after deducting anything that you owe us. Standing orders, direct debits and cards stop on the closing date, and you should make other arrangements in good time. These terms continue to apply to anything that happened before the account was closed.

## 17.8 General terms: Death of an account holder

When we are told that an account holder has died, we ask for a copy of the death certificate. From that moment we stop cards, cheques and digital access for the person who has died and cancel any power of attorney, because a power of attorney ends on death.

An account in the sole name of the person who has died is frozen. We continue to accept payments in. We pay out only to the person who is legally entitled to deal with the estate, on sight of the document that proves it, such as a grant of probate or letters of administration. Before that, on request and on sight of the invoice, we will pay the funeral costs and any inheritance tax directly from the account, as far as the balance allows.

For a small balance we may agree to pay the next of kin without a formal grant, against a signed indemnity. We decide the limit for this from time to time.

A joint account is not frozen. It passes to the surviving holder or holders, who can go on using it, and we remove the name of the person who has died.

We know that this is a difficult time. Our bereavement team will guide the family through each step, and a single notification is enough for all accounts that the person held with us.

## 17.9 General terms: Loss of capacity and insolvency

If we are told, with medical or legal evidence, that an account holder is no longer able to manage their own financial affairs, we stop acting on that person's own instructions. We then take instructions only from a person who has legal authority to act for them, such as an attorney under a lasting power of attorney or a guardian appointed by a court.

Until such a person is in place, we will continue to make regular payments that were set up earlier, such as rent, care fees and utility bills, where this is clearly in the account holder's interest. On a joint account, we may ask that the other holder and the legal representative act together.

If you are declared bankrupt, or enter a formal arrangement with your creditors, you must tell us. The law may give control of your account to a trustee. We follow the trustee's instructions and may freeze the account until we receive them. We may also close the account or change it to a basic account without overdraft.

If we hear of a bankruptcy petition against you, we may refuse payments out of the account, other than those that a court allows, until the petition has been decided.

Money in your accounts with us is protected by the official deposit insurance scheme of the country where your branch is located. If a bank is unable to repay its depositors, the scheme pays them compensation up to a limit set by law. The limit applies to each depositor per bank, not to each account, so all your accounts with us are added together. On a joint account each holder is treated as owning an equal share unless the account records show otherwise.

The scheme covers current accounts, savings accounts and fixed-term deposits. It does not cover investments, the contents of a safe deposit box or electronic money held with another firm. Money that you owe us is not set against your deposits when the compensation is worked out, unless the law of the scheme provides for it.

Temporarily high balances that result from certain life events, such as the sale of your home, an inheritance or an insurance payment, may be protected above the normal limit for a limited period.

The current limit, the name of the scheme and how to make a claim are set out in the information sheet that we give you when you open an account and once a year with your statement.
